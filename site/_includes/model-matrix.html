The model matrix, as an actual matrix: one row per model, one column per property, sortable and filterable. It replaced seven per-family sections on /docs/models/ that listed the same models as prose -- a hip-ep developer's review pointed out that a page called Model Matrix was a list, and that comparing two models meant opening two pages. The seven family pages are still there and still the place the architectural story is told. What changed is that they stopped being the navigation path: the Family cell links to them, so a reader who wants depth clicks once, and a reader comparing models never has to. Takes no parameters. Everything comes from _data/models.yml and _data/benchmarks.yml, so this table cannot claim a condition its caller invented. NO-JS FALLBACK. The table is fully rendered in source order and every row is visible; the controls above it are `hidden` until initModelMatrix() removes the attribute. Same arrangement as the deck and the card wall on the landing page: ship the readable thing, then upgrade it. Sorting and filtering are conveniences, not the only way to read this. SORT KEYS. Text columns sort on their own text. The three that must not are carried in `data-sort-value`: Parameters the leading total-parameter count as a number, so 9B sorts below 70B instead of above it -- `params` is a sentence ("35B total, 3B active") and its own text sorts alphabetically. Prefill the computed rate, as a number. Decode the measured rate, as a number. A row with no measurement gets `data-sort-value="-1"`, which parks it at the bottom ascending and the top descending -- deliberately, either way: a blank is not a zero and should not be sorted among real figures. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign snap = site.data.benchmarks.snapshot -%} {%- assign lens = site.data.benchmarks.prompt_lengths -%} {%- assign all_llm = site.data.models.llm -%} {%- assign all_vlm = site.data.models.vlm -%} {%- assign total = all_llm.size | plus: all_vlm.size -%} {%- comment -%} THE TWO MEASURED COLUMNS, AND WHY THEY ARE THESE TWO. Prefill @2K prompt tokens / TTFT at the 2048-token case, in tokens/second. Decode generated tokens/second at the 128-token case. Both are pinned to ONE prompt length across every row, and that is the whole point of putting them in a sortable column. The card wall on the landing page does something different -- it prints each model's BEST prefill rate, whichever length produced it, and labels which one. That is right for a card, which is read alone. It would be wrong here: a column readers can sort has to hold one condition, or the sort ranks models measured differently against each other and looks authoritative doing it. 2048 rather than 128 for prefill. At 128 tokens the figure is almost entirely fixed start-up cost and separates nothing; 2048 is where the rate is actually a rate. 16384 would also be defensible and is on /docs/benchmarks/ along with everything else -- this page shows one length, not three. Decode stays at 128 because it barely moves with prompt length: a few percent across the whole range on most rows. No length is printed in its header for the same reason -- see the same note in model-card.html. THE RATE IS DERIVED, NOT MEASURED. The dashboard reports TTFT and TPS; prefill tokens/second is prompt_tokens over TTFT, an average across the whole prefill including start-up, so it runs slightly pessimistic and exists nowhere upstream. Do not try to reconcile it with a dashboard column. The numerator is `prompt_tokens` -- tokens actually fed -- not `lens`, the nominal name of the case. For a vision-language model those differ by more than 15x because the image is tokenised into the prompt, so using the nominal length would not give a slightly wrong rate, it would give a meaningless one. CROSS-CLASS COMPARISON IS THE ONE THING THIS TABLE CANNOT DO HONESTLY. A VLM's prefill includes running the image through the vision encoder; a text model's does not. Sorting all sixteen by prefill therefore ranks two different amounts of work. That is what the Text / Vision-language filter is for, and there is a note under the table saying so. Do not remove either. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign i_prefill = 1 -%} {%- assign i_decode = 0 -%} {%- assign prefill_label = lens[i_prefill] | divided_by: 1024 | append: "K" -%}
